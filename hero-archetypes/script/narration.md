**Main argument:** Campbell noticed that the same hero keeps appearing in every culture. Popper and Deutsch help explain why: every person is a problem-solver, and in Deutsch's phrase a *universal explainer*. A world full of problem-solvers keeps telling stories about one. The hero distils what greatness looks like against a problem: sometimes wrong, never giving up hope or will.

## 2. The hero with a thousand faces

> In 1949, the mythologist Joseph Campbell published The Hero with a Thousand Faces.
> Reading myths from all over the world, he kept meeting the same figure: one hero, wearing different faces.
> Leave home. Face trials. Return changed.
> Carl Jung called recurring figures like this archetypes.
> Campbell's real discovery wasn't a formula. It was the recurrence itself.
> So why does every culture keep inventing the same hero? Hold that thought.

## 7. Why the hero keeps coming back

> So why does the same hero keep appearing?
> Campbell found the pattern. Karl Popper and David Deutsch help explain it.
> Popper argued that all life is problem solving. We guess, we make mistakes, and we correct them.
> Deutsch went further. Every person, he argues, is a universal explainer: able, in principle, to understand anything that can be understood.
> He put it in two lines. Problems are inevitable. Problems are soluble.
> A world full of problem-solvers keeps telling stories about one.
> That's why the thousand faces feel like one.
> The hero is what greatness looks like against a problem: wrong sometimes, tested always, and never out of hope.
