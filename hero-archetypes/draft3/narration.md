## 0. Cold open
[tag: Prologue]
[register: prologue]

> Before the first story was written, there was a fire.
> Not the fire of the forge or the hearth, but a smaller one, kindled in a single mind: the thought that the darkness could be understood.
> It passed from hand to hand.
> To the hunter who learned the herd.
> To the healer who learned the fever.
> To the child who asked *why*, and would not be silenced.
> Kingdoms forgot it.
> Empires burned the books that held it.
> And always, somewhere, someone found it again.
> They called the ones who carried it heroes.
> They gave them a thousand faces.
> *They were wrong about the number.*

## 1. Hook
[tag: The Question]
[register: scripture]

> The mythologist Joseph Campbell said the hero has a thousand faces.
> Decades before him, Carl Jung traced the same figure through the myths of the world.
> And they were right: there is a pattern.
> But the patterns they drew were **parochial**, not universal.
> The hero's variety is not a thousand.
> It is **infinite**.
> Every face is a costume, made for one place and one time.
> Beneath them all is one core: the knowledge of how to solve problems, reliably.

## 2. Map and territory
[tag: The Map]
[register: mythmaker]

> Stories are maps of heroism.
> This is about the **territory**.
> On Campbell's map, the hero leaves home, survives the ordeal and returns changed.
> Each stage is a shadow.
> Leaving home is meeting a problem no one has solved.
> The ordeal is the **error**.
> The return is new knowledge, carried back.
> The mentor is the wisdom of tradition.
> The villain is the problem, given a face.
> *The hero's gift is to see the dragon as a soluble problem.*
> To learn where it sleeps, why it burns, and make the knowledge that defeats it, until it is no problem at all.

## 3. Reaching for the Hero
[tag: Convergence]
[register: rational]

> Jung blamed inheritance: an archetype buried in us all.
> There is a better explanation.
> Eyes evolved separately in octopuses and in us.
> Wings evolved separately in insects, birds and bats.
> Seeing and flying are real problems, and good solutions to real problems look alike.
> The hero recurs for the same reason.
> Not inherited.
> **Rediscovered.**

## 4. The Hero is knowledge
[tag: Popper & Deutsch]
[register: rational]

> So what is the Hero?
> Not a plot.
> Not an archetype.
> **Knowledge.**
> Hope that problems are soluble.
> Humility about your own errors.
> Curiosity.
> Criticism.
> The courage to walk into the unknown.
> Running in a mind, it turns one cycle: a problem, a guess, an error, a correction.
> Karl Popper said all life is problem solving.
> David Deutsch calls every person a universal explainer.
> The Hero is that explainer at its best.

## 5. The levels
[tag: Infinite Faces]
[register: labyrinth]
[scene: stack]

> Think of it as Richard Dawkins thinks of a gene: not one strand, but every copy, everywhere.
> One copy of the Hero lives in one mind.
> All the copies in one world form a **lineage**, each different, each recognisable.
> And if Deutsch is right that there are many worlds, knowledge is what stays the same across them.
> The worlds scatter.
> The Hero lines up.
> Above it all is the truth: the Hero as it really is.
> Every copy reaches for it.
> None arrives.
> *Every answer opens new questions.*
> *The climb has no top.*

## 6. The fall
[tag: Guts]
[register: stark]

> Even the greatest hero falls.
> Guts loses everything, and answers with revenge.
> It is an error, and it nearly eats him alive.
> A hero's strength is not in never falling.
> It is in **correcting**, moral errors too.
> A hero stays a hero while the core survives: the part that can still say, *I was wrong*.
> The real fall comes when an idea seals itself off from criticism.

## 7. Open to criticism
[tag: No Exceptions]
[register: stark]

> So the core cannot be sealed either.
> A core beyond question is just another **dogma**.
> Everything stays open.
> The hero's methods.
> The hero's values.
> Even the nature of the Hero.
> *This is not a definition.*
> *It is a theory.*
> *It could be wrong.*

## 8. Parochial and universal
[tag: Nausicaä]
[register: rational]

> Most heroes are parochial.
> A scientist can be a hero in the lab and a dogmatist everywhere else.
> But knowledge connects in ways no one can predict.
> No wall against criticism holds: the errors leak through.
> So the true Hero is **universal**.
> Nausicaä does not just defend her valley.
> She studies the forest everyone else wants to burn, and finds the poison was in the soil.

## 9. Heroes and heroic acts
[tag: K]
[register: mythmaker]

> Every hero performs heroic acts.
> Not every heroic act comes from a hero.
> K learns he is no one in particular.
> No prophecy.
> No destiny.
> He does the heroic thing **anyway**.
> A person can find the path late, lose it, or stand on it for one moment before the old ideas return.
> That is why stories love the unlikely hero.

## 10. Traditions
[tag: Aragorn, Frodo & Sam]
[register: mythmaker]

> And no hero corrects **alone**.
> Aragorn inherits his ancestor's failure, and is raised under another name: Estel.
> Hope.
> Frodo fails at the fire, and the quest is saved by a mercy shown long before.
> Heroes begin traditions.
> Traditions raise heroes.
> A tradition of criticism may be the Hero living outside any one mind.

## 11. Turn to the viewer
[tag: The Frontier]
[register: rational]

> Most people are heroes somewhere.
> We only notice the ones who stand apart.
> In a dogmatic land, asking a question is heroic.
> Brecht's Galileo put it plainly: *unhappy the land that needs heroes.*
> A culture that learns these ideas has more heroes, and notices each one less.
> But the **frontier** moves.
> New problems arrive, and no one can know their kind.
> There is always an edge of the map.
> The greatest heroes cross it.

## 12. Close
[tag: Infinite Faces]
[register: prologue]

> Campbell saw a thousand faces.
> Behind them is one Hero, and its faces never run out.
[register: scripture]
> *The Hero recurs because it is the best solution to the one problem every life faces: how to solve problems.*
> The fire is still passing from hand to hand.
> Somewhere, someone is finding it again.
> It could be **you**.
